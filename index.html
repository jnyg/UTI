About the workshop

Integrable systems have deep connections with many areas of mathematics and theoretical physics, including quantum field theory, statistical mechanics, representation theory, geometry, and topology. In recent years, the field has broadened considerably, with new directions emerging at the interfaces between different areas of mathematics and theoretical physics.

Unbeaten Tracks in Integrability brings together researchers working along these different directions, with particular emphasis on connections between research traditions that have so far remained relatively unexplored. The workshop aims to create a setting in which new problems, structures, and methods can emerge from interaction across these boundaries.

Topics include

  • Quantum groups, the Yang–Baxter equation, and quantum integrable systems
  • The tetrahedron equation, higher-dimensional integrable models, and higher algebraic structures
  • Integrable field theories and sigma models, including integrable deformations
  • Four-dimensional Chern–Simons theory and its higher-dimensional generalizations
  • Integrable boundaries, defects, and interfaces
  • Integrability in string theory, gauge/gravity duality, and supersymmetric field theory
  • Quantum curves, dimer models, and related geometric methods
  • Integrable structures in conformal field theory, vertex algebras, algebraic geometry, and related areas

Vincent CaudrelierLagrangian multiforms, holomorphic-topological gauge theory and the Hitchin system+

After a brief review of Lagrangian multiform theory as a variational framework to describe integrable systems, I will focus on Lagrangian one-forms which deal with finite dimensional integrable systems. Introducing a gauging procedure within this framework allows for a variational description of the well-established Hamiltonian reduction technique to produce integrable hierarchies. This enables us to adapt Hitchin's original construction (which I will briefly review) to the Lagrangian setting and to obtain for the first time the variational description of the Hitchin system. It turns out that doing so unravels a direct connection with holomorphic-topological gauge theory which has triggered a lot of attention as a gauge theoretic framework to construct integrable theories. Specifically, in our case, we construct a gauged Lagrangian one-form for the so-called 3d mixed BF theory with defects. Special cases of our construction yield Lagrangian one-forms for (perhaps) more familiar integrable systems such as (elliptic) Gaudin and spin Calogero models. This is based on joint work with D. Harland, A.A. Singh and B. Vicedo.

Lewis ColeClassical Soliton Scattering in 2+1d Integrable Field Theory+

This talk describes classical soliton scattering in the 2+1d integrable chiral model, related to the Bogomolny monopole equation and self-dual Yang-Mills. In addition to lump solitons, localised around points in space, this theory admits extended line solitons, localised around lines in space. The classical scattering of these solitons can be computed exactly using integrability, given in terms of maps between incoming and outgoing soliton data. Furthermore, multi-soliton interactions factorise into pairwise processes and the result is independent of the order of interactions. I will discuss potential connections to the Yang-Baxter and tetrahedron equations, and show how 3d line soliton scattering reproduces known results from the 1+1d sine-Gordon model.

Zhengping GuiA Virasoro Central Extension via Higher Chiral Operations+

Faonte, Hennion, and Kapranov classified higher-dimensional Kac–Moody central extensions using the Jouanolou model for the derived global sections of punctured affine space. In this talk, we use this model to study higher-dimensional chiral algebras. Using higher chiral operations, we construct a distinguished Virasoro central extension in higher dimensions. This is based on joint work with Minghao Wang and Brian Williams, as well as ongoing work with Brian Williams.

Rei Inoue3D integrability from quantum cluster algebras+

The tetrahedron equation was proposed by Zamolodchikov in 1980, and the 3D reflection equation was proposed by Isaev and Kulish in 1997, to describe the integrability of 3D lattice models. In this talk I introduce solutions to these equations by using quantum cluster mutations, based on joint works with Atsuo Kuniba, Xiaoyue Sun, Yuji Terashima and Junya Yagi. We develop the method introduced by Sun and Yagi in 2022, where they attached three types of quivers to wiring diagrams, and for each of them the quantum mutations yield the solution of the tetrahedron equation in terms of quantum dilogarithm functions. Our strategy is to embed the noncommuting algebra generated by quantum y-variables in the q-Weyl algebra associated with wiring diagrams. This approach enables a unified understanding of various solutions to the tetrahedron equation that were previously known separately.

Atsuo KunibaQuantized six-vertex model+

The recently introduced quantized six-vertex (Q6V) model is a six-vertex model whose Boltzmann weights take values in a q-Weyl algebra and satisfy tetrahedron equations. It can be viewed either as a quantum integrable system on two-dimensional admissible wiring diagrams or as a three-dimensional classical system with commuting layer transfer matrices depending on two spectral parameters.

The Q6V model has several notable features, including its connections to quantum cluster algebras, dimer models, the relativistic Toda chain, and related topics. My main focus will be on the quantum Frobenius property at roots of unity, which leads to a far-reaching generalization of the free-parafermion spectra found in Baxter's 1989 clock model associated with the superintegrable chiral Potts model.

(Joint work with Rei Inoue, Yuji Terashima, and Junya Yagi.)

Norton Lee\(Q\)-operators, cluster mutations, and codimension-two defects+

We construct Baxter \(Q\)-operators for a family of quantum cluster integrable systems associated with quivers. A distinguished sequence of quantum cluster mutations yields an ordered product of Faddeev's quantum dilogarithms. We prove that these operators form a commuting family and that their shifted quotients are transfer matrices whose coefficients are commuting Hamiltonians. We identify these transfer matrices with matrix elements of products of explicit local Lax matrices of rank two. Local cluster transformations move flavor insertions along the chain while preserving their ordered flavor parameters and the transfer matrix. The unflavored specialization recovers the open \(q\)-deformed Toda chain. We also derive integral kernels for the \(Q\)-operators and evaluate an associated Fourier transform as a factorized scalar \(Q\)-function. Finally, we relate the quivers to the BPS quivers of five-dimensional N=1 gauge theories in the decoupling limit and compare the normalized scalar \(Q\)-function with the perturbative contribution of a canonical codimension-two defect wrapping a squashed three-sphere.

George LinardopoulosString integrability of defect CFT and dynamical reflection matrices+

The D3-D5 and D2-D4 probe-brane systems with nonzero worldvolume flux are holographically dual to N=4 super Yang-Mills and ABJM theory in the presence of half-BPS domain walls. The two domain wall systems are thought to be integrable; the evidence comes mainly from the study of correlation functions at weak coupling. In the present talk we show that the string theory duals of these systems are classically integrable. In other words, the string boundary conditions on the probe branes preserve the integrability of the corresponding Green-Schwarz sigma models. This finding suggests that the dual domain wall systems are integrable to all loop orders and for any value of the bond dimension.

Joaquin LiniadoHolography for integrable field theories+

Since its introduction, four-dimensional Chern–Simons theory has offered a unifying origin for classically integrable field theories in two dimensions. However, the quantum aspects of this construction have remained largely mysterious. In this talk I will present a first step in exploring this direction, based on a holographic construction in topological string theory.

Alexander SchenkelHomological algebra of integrability I: The framework+

Costello and Yamazaki discovered a relationship between two-dimensional integrable field theories and four-dimensional topological-holomorphic Chern-Simons theories. In this talk, I will explain how this relationship admits a rigorous and conceptually clean description in terms of the homotopy theory of \(L_\infty\)-algebras. Combining techniques from complex geometry with homotopy transfer, I will show how to extract the associated integrable field theory from a topological-holomorphic Chern-Simons theory, offering a new perspective on Lax connections in terms of \(\infty\)-morphisms. I will conclude by illustrating how the use of higher Lie algebras as structure algebras extends this framework to arbitrary dimensions, providing structural insights into higher-dimensional integrability.

This talk is based on joint work with M. Benini and B. Vicedo [arXiv:2601.19993] and with M. Benini, R. Cullinan and B. Vicedo [arXiv:2604.24864].

Benoît VicedoHomological algebra of integrability II: Computations+

The Costello-Yamazaki construction, relating 4d Chern-Simons theory to 2d integrable field theories, admits a homotopy-algebraic formulation in terms of cyclic \(L_\infty\)-algebras. In this language, the procedure of ``integrating out'' the spectral curve \(\mathbb{C}P^1\) is formalised as homotopy transfer of the cyclic \(L_\infty\)-algebra underlying 4d Chern-Simons theory to its partial Dolbeault cohomology along \(\mathbb{C}P^1\). The key ingredient in making this procedure computationally explicit is the construction of strong deformation retracts for divisor-twisted Dolbeault complexes on \(\mathbb{C}P^1\). As a concrete application, I will show how the action functional of the principal chiral model with a Wess-Zumino term, together with its Lax connection, can be recovered from 4d Chern-Simons theory.

This talk is based on joint work with M. Benini, R. Cullinan and A. Schenkel [arXiv:2607.12142], and work in progress.

Junbao WuThree-point functions in Aharony-Bergman-Jafferis-Maldacena theory and integrable boundary states+

We investigate the correlators of three single-trace operators in Aharony-Bergman-Jafferis-Maldacena (ABJM) theory from the perspective of integrable boundary states. Specifically, we focus on scenarios where two operators are 1/3-BPS and the entire correlation function is considered within the twisted-translated frame. The correlator can be expressed as the overlap between a boundary state and a Bethe state. It is found that the boundary state formed by the two 1/3-BPS operators is integrable when the number of Wick contractions between the non-BPS operator and one of the 1/3-BPS operators is 0 or 1. We compute the overlaps for the proven integrable cases utilizing the symmetries and the coordinate Bethe ansatz.
